# P2-T40 — Link a paper's attached documents and versions, never merge (and the second top-up of twenty pairs, item 370(d))

**Findings.** The owner decided on 2026-11-11 that the documents attached to one paper (reviewer reports, editor's assessments, decision letters, author responses, Faculty Opinions recommendations) and the versions of one F1000Research article are separate works that point at each other ("linked versions"), not one work. The duplicate detector (the "dedup engine") now does exactly that: when two records carry different identifiers from one paper's identifier family (a DOI, the permanent identifier a publisher gives a document, whose "family stem" names the paper), the engine keeps both records as separate works and links the two; the same rule covers two F1000Research reports on two versions of one article. The family decides first, so a shared title or a shared PubMed number can no longer make two such documents one work, and no third record can join the two documents into one group. The comparison that finds these documents across different title groups stays. The code that only served the old merging (a review yields its fields to the paper; the newest F1000Research version wins the DOI and the date) is removed, with dated notes. Over the 552 pairs of the measuring collection exactly 56 outcomes changed, every one from "merged" to "linked"; no pair the owner labelled "same paper" stopped being merged, and no pair labelled "different" became merged or linked. On the owner's 70 labelled pairs precision rose from 0.6667 (13 false merges, target not met) to **1.0000 with no false merge**, interval 0.8713 to 1.0000, still too few pairs to confirm the 0.99 target; recall is unchanged at 0.9630, interval 0.8172 to 0.9934, also undecided. The second job then drew **twenty more pairs** for the owner to label, by the method written into the literature-database document first, under the seed "P2-T13 label sample top-up 2 2026-11-12"; the sample file now holds numbered top-up blocks, and the first block is unchanged byte for byte. Until the owner labels the twenty, the accuracy report refuses to measure, by design. Every static check, the unit tests and the build pass; all 12 deliberate faults planted in the new code were caught. Three consecutive integration runs inside real Zotero were clean, 355 tests passed each time. Two side effects in the Zotero layer were measured and left alone (section 10).

## 1. The rule and where the rule is documented

- **The owner's decision:** `plan/00` section 6 item 370(a)–(c), decided 2026-11-11.
- **The rule:** `docs/02` section 11.3, the note "Decided 2026-11-11 by the owner, written 2026-11-12": six numbered points.
  1. Tier 0 (the exact-DOI tier, the first the engine applies) gives two different DOIs of one family a **link** at confidence 0.97, evidence `rule: "doi-family"` and the stem. Equal DOIs still match, so one document from two sources is still one work.
  2. **Why the family wins over every later tier** (stated in the note): the engine stops at the first tier that decides, and Tier 0 runs first. Without that order, two same-titled reports (Wiley's `…/v1/review1` and `…/v1/review2`, both `Review for "…"`; two Faculty Opinions recommendations of one article) would be an exact-title match, turned into a link only because their DOIs happen to conflict. A pair sharing a PubMed number, a PubMed Central number, an arXiv number or a Semantic Scholar number would be a **match** across the conflicting DOIs. A shared DOI stem is the publisher's own statement that the two are different documents of one paper.
  3. Two F1000Research reports on two versions of one article are still compared on their titles without the version note; a match there becomes a link at the same tier and confidence.
  4. The group-level check (which already stopped a third record joining a preprint to the version the preprint names) now also keeps two family documents apart. Their link keeps its own decision and carries `groupSplit: true`.
  5. The family comparison pass stays. Its cost becomes n(n − 1)/2 comparisons for a family of n records, not n − 1.
  6. The library matcher shares the decision function, so an incoming family document is reported as a link to the library item, not a match.
- **The removed merge rules:** `docs/07` section 5.1, the note "Superseded 2026-11-12". `docs/02` section 11.4 gains a superseded note for the F1000Research versions.
- Earlier notes are kept and marked superseded where the notes no longer apply: the 2026-11-10 family note, the family-pass cost clause, and the 2026-11-11 one-top-up rule.

## 10. Seen but not fixed

1. **A family of three or more is only partly related in Zotero** (measured). The pipeline step records one related identifier per kind, the first partner seen (`relate` in `src/pipeline/searchImport/stages.ts`). The rule the importer uses to relate items and batch writes reads those identifiers (`areOtherVersions` in `src/model/merge.ts`). An article, its decision letter and its author response give three works and three engine links. Two of the three pairs are related, but the decision letter and the author response are not (`areOtherVersions` false), so the importer would not relate those two items. The doc comment on `areOtherVersions` ("true of every linked pair of one run") is wrong for such families. This predates the card; the card makes the limitation reachable for review documents.
2. **A review document imported alone gets a misleading extra-field line** (measured). When only one side of a link is imported, the item mapper writes the partner's DOI as "Preprint DOI:" on anything that is not a preprint (`toZoteroMapping` in `src/zotero/itemMapper.ts`). An author response imported alone therefore reads "Preprint DOI: 10.7554/elife.85605", naming its own article as its preprint. The same happens the other way round. The wording belongs to `docs/07` section 6.2 and is a decision.
3. **Repeating `--extend=N` without `--seed=` draws another block.** The default seed carries the next block number. This is what the brief asked for (only a repeated seed is refused), but an accidental second run would append a block. Undoing one is a hand edit of the sample file.
4. The scaffold tool reports a newer version, 0.9.2 to 0.9.4; not acted on.
