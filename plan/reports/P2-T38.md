# P2-T38: Merge a paper's reviews and versions (the DOI family). Report

**Findings.** The deduplication engine now treats the documents of one paper as one work. A DOI is the permanent identifier a publisher gives a document. When two DOIs share a "family stem" in one of five publisher forms, the records merge. These cover review documents: eLife sub-articles, transparent peer review reports, Faculty Opinions recommendations and F1000Research peer review reports. They also cover F1000Research article versions. All eleven pairs the owner named are now merged. The stop condition was not hit: no pair labelled `not-duplicate` or `linked-version` became merged. Across all 534 corpus pairs, 38 outcomes change, and all 38 move to merged. Eleven of the 38 are the owner's labelled duplicates; the other 27 are unlabelled and are listed below. Ordinary journal-article imports do not change: all 940 record groups whose members did not change give byte-identical merged works before and after the change. All five checks pass. Three consecutive integration runs each gave 355 passed, 0 failed, 5 pending. Nothing is committed.

## 1. The rule and where it is documented

- **`docs/02-literature-database-apis.md` §11.3**, note "Decided 2026-11-10 (owner, plan/00 §6 item 362(b)): the documents of one paper are one work". It has a table of the five DOI forms and two rules:
  - **Rule 1:** two different DOIs with the same family stem are a match at Tier 0, rule `doi-family`, confidence 0.97. The §11.5 rule 1 "conflicting DOI" downgrade no longer applies to them.
  - **Rule 2:** two F1000Research peer review reports on different versions of one article (per-version DOIs with different stems) do not record a DOI conflict. Their titles are compared with the "[version N; peer review: …]" note removed.
  - Neither rule adds a comparison. The note also records the exclusions: eLife version DOIs are not a family, and F1000 sibling journals are not listed. It ends with the measured figures.
- **`docs/02` §11.4**, an added note: F1000Research versions of one article are merged. Gate G-16 is unchanged for every other preprint and published pair, including an eLife reviewed preprint and its version of record.
- **`docs/07-architecture-and-data-model.md` §5.1**, note "a review document yields to its article". Gate G-16 is not touched. No type and no preference changes.
- Code: `doiFamilyStem`, `sameDoiFamily`, `isReviewDocumentDoi` and `isPerVersionReviewDoi` in `src/model/ids.ts`. In `src/sources/shared/dedupe.ts`: `DOI_TIER`, `F1000_VERSION_NOTE`, `arePeerReviewReportsAcrossVersions` and `compareCandidates`. In `src/model/merge.ts`: `isReviewDocument`, `canonicalOrder` and `merge`. In `src/pipeline/searchImport/stages.ts`: `mergeGroups`.

## 2. Winner policy and the coordinator-decision text

**Article plus its reviews or responses.** The article wins. The records that are not review documents supply every field: title, type, authors, dates, venue and abstract. The only exceptions are the `ids` and `relatedVersionIds` unions. `ids` keeps the article's DOI and lists each review DOI as a conflict, and `provenance.recordIds` still names every record. Zotero receives the article. The search preview's representative row is the first record in fetch order that is not a review document.

Recorded in `docs/07` §5.1 (and cross-referenced from `docs/02` §11.4):

> **DECIDED 2026-11-10 (coordinator, under item 313): which record wins when no article is in the group.** The existing policy above applies unchanged, because nothing in the owner's decision asks for more and every other choice would need a new rule. (a) **Two reviewer reports**, or any group of review documents only: the longest title that is not truncated, the DOI of the first record in the canonical order (source precedence, then record id ascending), the earliest complete date and the most complete author list; the other reports' DOIs are kept as conflicts in `ids`. (b) **Two versions of one F1000Research article**: the same policy, so on the 2026-11-10 corpus the item takes version 1's DOI (its Crossref record sorts first by record id) and version 1's date (the earliest), the longest title (version 2's, as PubMed prints it), and PubMed's PMID; version 2's DOI is kept as a conflict. Groups of ordinary journal articles are unaffected: over the 534-pair corpus all 940 groups whose members did not change give byte-identical merged works before and after `P2-T38`. Whether the newest version should win instead is the owner's to decide (`plan/reports/P2-T38.md`).

## 9. Seen but not fixed

1. **Family members in two different title groups are never compared.** Before comparing titles, the engine sorts records into groups of similar titles. These records sit in different groups, so they stay apart:
   - eLife 85605.sa0 stays alone;
   - the 92046 group holds sa1–sa3 but not sa0;
   - the 98867 reviewer reports and the 98867 assessments stay two groups.

   Closing this needs a new pass that compares records by DOI family, plus a matching new origin in `candidatePairs`, the corpus script's list of how pairs are found. That would add pairs to the fixture, so it is the owner's decision.
2. **The check against the user's existing Zotero library** (`matchAgainstLibrary`) cannot look up a DOI family. A review of a paper already in the library is therefore still imported as a separate item.
3. **Some forms are not covered yet:** Wiley decision-letter and author-response DOI forms, and F1000 sibling journals such as Wellcome Open Research. F1000 peer review reports also do not merge with their F1000 article, because the report DOI (10.5256) and the article DOI (10.12688) have different stems.
4. **Five dependency-edge defects predate this card:**
   - P2-T27 says it blocks P2-T14 and P2-T16, but neither lists P2-T27 among its dependencies;
   - P2-T27 depends on P2-T01, but P2-T01 does not list P2-T27 in its Blocks;
   - P2-T32 says it blocks P2-T14 and P2-T16, but neither lists P2-T32 among its dependencies.

   P2-T38's own edges are consistent.
5. **The search preview's representative row changes** for groups that contain review documents: the row is now the article, not whichever record was fetched first. Only those groups change.
6. **Recall stays undecided** at 34 true duplicates in the sample. Confirming the 0.90 target needs 35, so the sample is one pair short.
