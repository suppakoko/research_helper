## PLOS and Oxford University Press candidate fixtures: 12 candidates and 4 reserves found, 3 articles excluded for non-commercial licences

I checked every article below on its publisher landing page in this session. No PDF was fetched, no file was written, no page asked for verification, and nothing needed the PubMed Central fallback.

**Caveat on the quotes:** WebFetch reads pages through a summarising model, and that model splits long quotes into pieces under 125 characters. The licence quotes and heading lists below are what it returned, so a human should check them by eye before using the articles as test fixtures.

**Column counts** are "expected from journal style" throughout. None was seen on a page.

**PDF links:** PLOS links are relative to https://journals.plos.org and Oxford University Press links are relative to https://academic.oup.com. All were copied from the landing pages and none was opened.

### What could not be verified

- **Column counts:** none was seen on a page. Brain Communications is the least certain (single or two columns), followed by GigaScience.
- **Supplementary material appended to the PDF:** no landing page shows this. Oxford University Press supplements are linked as separate files (a ZIP for SCARAP, a separate PDF for both PNAS Nexus articles). PLOS supporting information is listed as separate files. Only the two Monthly Notices articles have content after the References that belongs to the article itself (appendices in candidate 1).
- **Licence quotes on Oxford University Press pages:** the summarising model returned only the start of the sentence plus the licence link. The PLOS quotes were returned in full, in pieces.
- **Heading lists:** for long pages, the later headings were partly taken from the page's "Article Contents" menu rather than read in the body text. This applies to the HD 28471 article (sections 11 onward), GigaScience and PNAS Nexus.
- **"Significance Statement"** in both PNAS Nexus articles appears in the body text but not in the Article Contents menu.
